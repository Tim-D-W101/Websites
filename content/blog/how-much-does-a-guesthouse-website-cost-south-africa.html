What guesthouse websites typically cost in South Africa

There is no official national price list. “A website” can mean a few simple pages, or a site with live availability and online payments.

Looking at packages South African designers had published in 2026, simple starter sites often start from about R3 000 to R5 500 once-off. Mid-range sites with room pages, photos, rates and a WhatsApp or enquiry form commonly fall between about R5 000 and R12 000. Sites with live booking usually cost more, and the booking system itself is a separate monthly fee.

Two published examples: GHASA lists small websites from R4 500 once-off, with hosting from R150 a month and maintenance from R500. Host Genie lists a starter at R5 500 once-off, and a booking-ready site from R9 500, with booking-engine fees separate. Always ask what is included, and what you will pay after the first year.

What drives the cost

A two-room B&B can often sit on a few clear pages. A larger guesthouse with several room types, an area guide and a gallery takes more work. If you already have good photos and can describe your rooms, the build is simpler. If the designer has to write the copy or sort photos, that can add to the quote.

How guests book also matters. An enquiry form and a WhatsApp button is the simplest setup. Live availability and a “Book now” button, connected to a system like NightsBridge, is a bigger job. Payments, specials, reviews and a tight deadline all add time. Some quotes cover only the build, with hosting and later changes billed separately.

Once-off versus monthly

Most South African designers offer a once-off build, a monthly plan, or both.

With a once-off price, you pay for the work, then a smaller yearly fee to keep the site online. You pay again when you want changes, unless those are included. A monthly plan usually means you pay less at the start, with hosting and a set number of changes often included. Check the minimum term, what happens if you cancel, and whether you still own the site and the domain.

If you rarely change rates or photos, once-off can be cheaper over a few years. If you update specials often, a plan with inclusive changes can save you chasing small invoices.

Hidden costs to watch for

A .co.za domain is a small annual fee from a South African registrar. Make sure it is registered in your name, not the designer’s. Hosting keeps the site online and secure. Some quotes include the first year. Others add a monthly fee from the start. If hosting is not paid, the site can go offline. Updates to rates, photos and specials may also be extra, and email isn’t always included.

If guests will check dates and book on your site, you will usually pay a booking system as well as the website. NightsBridge publishes plans from R660 a month, excluding VAT, for 1 to 5 rooms. There is no NightsBridge setup fee, and they don’t charge commission on bookings that come through the button on your own website. Your designer may charge once-off to connect it. Card payments carry their own charges, billed by the bank or payment provider. Get all of this in writing on the quote.

DIY or a professional site?

You can build a site yourself on a website builder. That can be cheaper at the start, if you have the time to learn it, write the pages and keep it updated. The trade-off is time, and sometimes a site that is slow on a phone or hard for Google to find. A professional site costs more upfront, but you get a fixed scope and a site ready for guests who are already searching for you.

How a website can save you booking-site commission

Booking sites are good at sending new guests. They charge a commission or service fee on each booking they send. The exact rate depends on the platform and your agreement:

  • Booking.com does not publish one fixed rate for every property. It says the percentage depends on your property type, country and location, and is set out in your agreement. Visibility programmes can add to that.
  • Airbnb says most hosts on its single service fee structure pay 15.5%.
  • LekkeSlaap publishes a 15% commission, excluding VAT, on every confirmed booking, plus a once-off activation fee of R600 excluding VAT.

You don’t have to leave these sites. Keep them for new guests. Your own guesthouse website is for returning guests, referrals and Google searchers who want to book direct.

As an example, not a promise: if a guest pays R1 200 a night, a 15% fee is R180. Two direct nights a week would be about R18 720 a year that stays with you. Use your own rate and commission to do the sums.

What to ask a designer before you sign

  1. What pages and features are included, and is the price fixed?
  2. Is booking-system integration included, or quoted separately?
  3. Who owns the domain and the website once it is paid for?
  4. What do hosting and the domain cost after the first year?
  5. What does a change cost, and how quickly is it done?
  6. Is email included?
  7. Do I have to stop using Booking.com, Airbnb or LekkeSlaap? (You should not have to.)
  8. How long will the build take once you have my photos and content?
  9. Can I see a sample of my site before I pay for the full build?

If a quote is much cheaper than the ranges above, check what has been left out.
